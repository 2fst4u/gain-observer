# Ionosphere coefficient provenance

`src/physics/ionosphere/data/*.json` are generated by
`iri-build/build_coefficients.py` (standard library only) from the coefficient
files in the PyIRI 0.1.7 source distribution:

- Archive: `pyiri-0.1.7.tar.gz` from PyPI (`pip download --no-deps --no-binary :all: PyIRI==0.1.7`)
- SHA-256: `f30098cfaf73a916f8363e752f1f1bfb2015b19b915bf707b797640c473300c3`
- Licence: MIT, Copyright (c) 2023 victoriyaforsythe (see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`)

Regenerate with:

```
python3 iri-build/build_coefficients.py <extracted archive>/PyIRI/coefficients
```

## What is used

| Output | Input | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `month-MM.json` `foF2` | `URSI/ursi(MM+10).asc` | URSI-88 foF2 maps (IRI's default foF2 option), 13 × 76 terms at IG12 = 0 and 100 |
| `month-MM.json` `m3000` | `CCIR/ccir(MM+10).asc`, after its foF2 block | CCIR-67 M(3000)F2 maps, 9 × 49 terms at IG12 = 0 and 100 |
| `igrf.json` | `IGRF/IGRF13.shc`, epochs 2015, 2020, 2025 | IGRF-13 Gauss coefficients to degree 13 |

The conversion was checked value-for-value against PyIRI's own
`read_ccir_ursi_coeff` (zero difference for every month).

## Origin of the maps

The CCIR (ITU-R Report 340) and URSI-88 maps are the coefficient sets the
International Reference Ionosphere has always distributed; PyIRI redistributes
them. The IRI licence (<https://irimodel.org/IRI-2020/00_iri-License.txt>)
grants use, copying and modification of the IRI software and its
documentation; it does not mention the coefficient files separately. IGRF-13
is published by IAGA Working Group V-MOD for free use.

IGRF-13 covers 1900–2025. Later dates extrapolate linearly from 2020–2025, as
PyIRI does; at 300 km the resulting inclination error is a small fraction of a
degree, far below what the monthly-median maps resolve. Moving to IGRF-14
needs only a newer `.shc` file and the epoch list in the script.

## Test fixture

`tests/fixtures/pyiri-reference.json` holds 40 cases from PyIRI 0.1.7
`IRI_density_1day(..., ccir_or_ursi=1)` (three dates in 2026, F10.7 70, 150
and 220, five UTs, eight locations, every ninth case kept). Each PyIRI run
included a ring of points under the sun, because PyIRI normalises the F1
blending weight by its maximum over the whole evaluated grid; the ring makes
that maximum the cap of 10, which is what the single-point port uses. The
port matches PyIRI to about 1e-14 relative.
